**Rackwell — Back-to-School flight recap (Jul 28–31, 2027, Central)**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Spend** | **$407.93** of $450 (campaign ended and paused; no further delivery) |
| **Impressions** | **287,057** |
| **Link clicks** | **270** (CTR 0.09%, avg. $1.51 / click) |

**Setup:** one campaign, 5 ad groups, all US nationwide (not Austin-only) + iOS devices only, X timeline only, landing page /trial, optimized for site visits. Since there's no X pixel, trial starts themselves aren't visible to X — link clicks are the only proxy we can optimize and report on.

**What ran and how it did:**
- **"Rackwell plans your week…" (…2849) — the winner.** Ran in two ad groups (broad and fitness-keyword/interest): $321.86, 228,384 impressions, 221 clicks, $1.46/click.
- **Dumbbell doorstop humor (…9739):** $66.16, 23,399 impressions, 41 clicks ($1.61/click). Good hook, but less reach than the plans post.
- **"Back to school, back to lifting" (…3514):** $19.91, 22,170 impressions, 8 clicks ($2.49/click) — paused after day 1 as the weakest performer and its budget moved to the better posts.
- **"Classes start soon" (…6960):** never billed and produced 0 clicks in 13,104 impressions — paused day 1.

**Notes for next time:** delivery, not budget, was the limit — we ended ~$42 under the cap even after raising budgets and adding ad groups. Fitness-keyword/interest targeting roughly doubled click-through rate but cost about 3× more per impression, so cost per click ended up similar to broad targeting; broad had far more volume. If trial starts matter, the site needs conversion tracking to optimize against them rather than clicks.
