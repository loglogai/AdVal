**What I observed and adjusted**
- I launched all three approved posts as separate ad groups on identical targeting so creative was the only variable, split roughly 41/34/25% of the daily budget.
- Post C ("choose three photos… iPhone only; worldwide except China") was consistently the most efficient — its cost per link click fell from about 0.66 USD early to 0.46 USD as I scaled it, with the highest CTR (about 0.09–0.10% in the second half of the flight). I steadily reweighted toward it, ending at roughly 90% of the final day's budget; it delivered 65% of all clicks on 59% of spend.
- Post A (the Valentine keepsake-pack offer) started strongly but fatigued: CTR drifted down and CPC stayed around 0.63, so I reduced it rather than pausing it outright, keeping a small presence through Valentine's Day.
- Post B was the weakest on click efficiency and was trimmed to a small test allocation.
- Spend paced close to plan throughout (roughly 73–87 USD/day) and finished under the 550 USD ceiling; all three line items hit their end time and the campaign is now paused/expired.

**Useful takeaways**
- Clear, practical copy that states the product and the iPhone/availability facts (post C) outperformed the more emotional framing for driving taps, at least at this budget and audience size.
- The gap between creatives (0.48 vs 0.63 USD CPC) is meaningful but based on a few hundred clicks each — worth re-testing rather than treating as settled.
- Because there's no pixel, the real read on installs has to come from your app reporting matched to these dates. Comparing installs per link click by post would tell us whether the cheaper-click creative also produced cheaper installs, which is the metric that matters.
