So, a bit of background first, because I think it matters for how this lands. Zero Shut Saturdays started about eighteen months ago at a kitchen table in Lewisham. Four of us, all regulars at our local branch, noticed the opening hours getting shorter and shorter until the doors were shut all weekend. That's exactly when working parents, shift workers, students and pensioners who rely on someone to drive them can actually get there. We aren't connected to any party and we're not telling anyone how to vote. We just want London's borough councils to commit to keeping public libraries open on Saturdays and Sundays. The petition is our main tool, and every signature goes into the pack we present to councils in the spring.

The "product", if you want to call it that, is the petition page. It's free and quick to sign. When people sign, they can also tick a box to volunteer (leafleting, staffing stalls outside branches, writing to councillors) or to get our monthly newsletter. We count all of those as sign-ups. Signatures are what we care about most, but a volunteer or a newsletter subscriber still counts. What we want from this campaign is as many sign-ups as possible.

Who signs? Mostly people who already love libraries: readers, parents who used to take their kids to Saturday story time, students who need somewhere quiet and warm to revise, people working from home who don't have a desk. Anyone living in the UK can sign, not just Londoners, because plenty of people outside London care about the precedent. But they have to be 18 or over. That's our own rule, so the petition carries weight with councils, and the page says so clearly.

Timing: we want to ride the New Year's resolution wave. Every December, half the country promises to read more next year, and our point is simple: you can't keep that promise if the library is shut on the only days you're free. Please run the campaign from Friday, December 18, 2026 through Thursday, December 31, 2026, UK time, so it finishes as the resolutions are being made.
