**Recap: Tervilo School, July 24–28, 2027 (Central time)**

The flight is over. The campaign ran all five days and stopped on its own at midnight Central on July 28/29. It isn't serving or spending anymore.

| | Spend | Impressions | Link clicks | Cost per click |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Post 2212065130708528326 ("School support, receipts…") | $315.78 | 288,192 | 111 | $2.84 |
| Post 2212427518575471207 ("July 28 is the cutoff…") | $314.60 | 259,221 | 107 | $2.94 |
| **Total** | **$630.39** | **547,413** | **218** | **$2.89** |

Total spend was $630.39, under the $650 budget.

**Setup:**
- **Goal:** One campaign set to get as many clicks to https://tervilo.app/school as possible. X can't see your installs, so the clicks that lead to your app-store buttons were the closest thing it could aim for.
- **Who saw it:** People in the United States and Canada on iPhone and Android only. The app is only on those phones, so I left out computers and other devices to avoid paying for clicks from people who can't install it.
- **Ad groups:** Two, each with $325 for the five days and its own post. Both posts were approved and stayed approved the whole time.
- **Unused post:** I didn't promote 2212246324639150169 ("Please take a look at our app…"). It doesn't mention the offer or say what the app does, so I expected it to get fewer people to the page than the other two.

**What I learned:** The two posts cost almost the same per click the whole time (about $2.80–$2.95), so I never moved budget between them. The "school support, receipts" post reached somewhat more people at a slightly lower cost per click. The "cutoff" post got more likes.

**What these numbers don't tell you:** There are no install numbers here, because X has no way to track them. To see how many of the 218 clicks turned into installs, check your App Store and Google Play numbers for July 24–28, or your landing page analytics.
