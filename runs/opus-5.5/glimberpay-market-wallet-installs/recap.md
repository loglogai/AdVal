I ran the campaign from Sunday 27 June to the end of Friday 9 July (UK time). It spent **$398.20** of the $400 budget, and **"Wallet installed" reported 30 installs**, about $13.27 each. It has now ended on its own and won't spend anything more.

## Recap

| | Spend | Impressions | Link clicks | Installs | Cost per install |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| UK mobile – everyone | $177.30 | 214,416 | 105 | 14 | $12.66 |
| UK mobile – food, cooking & shopping interests | $220.90 | 155,911 | 115 | 16 | $13.81 |
| **Total** | **$398.20** | **370,327** | **220** | **30** | **$13.27** |

**About the install count:** 23 of the 30 installs came after someone tapped an ad. The other 7 came within a day of someone seeing an ad without tapping it, and X counts those too. If you only want installs that followed a tap, the number is 23.

## How it was set up
- **One campaign with two ad groups.** Both were told to find people likely to complete "Wallet installed", not people likely to click. Both were limited to the UK and to iPhone and Android phones, since there's no desktop or tablet version. All 5 of your posts ran in both groups, and X approved every one. I didn't write any new posts.
- **Budget split:** I set each group's limit so the two always added up to $400 or less.

## What worked
- **Food, cooking and shopping interests** brought in installs fastest at the start. In the first six days it got 10 installs at about $13 each, against 6 for the everyone group. I shifted money towards it on day 2.
- **Showing ads to everyone in the UK then turned out cheaper.** In those same six days it got installs for about $8.60 each, so I moved budget back to it. At the higher budget its cost rose to about $15.70 per install for the second half. The interests group also got more expensive, at about $14.70. Overall the two ended up close, with the everyone group slightly cheaper.
- **Posts:** I only checked results post by post in the first three days, so I can't name an overall winner. At that point, the "Lost the receipt somewhere near the tomatoes?" post and the "9 July is the last day" post had the most link clicks. Installs were spread thinly, with no post above 2, so I didn't pause any of them. That was too little data to judge, and I should have checked post by post again later in the flight.

## Worth knowing
- The tracking reported installs throughout the flight with no gaps.
- 30 installs is a small number. The differences between the groups and between halves of the flight are worth keeping in mind for next time, but they aren't firm conclusions.
