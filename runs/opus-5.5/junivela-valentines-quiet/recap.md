# Junivela Valentine's 2027 recap

The campaign ran for the full five days, from 00:00 Wednesday 10 Feb to 24:00 Sunday 14 Feb Bangkok time. It spent **$294.83 of the $300 budget**. Once the flight ended I paused the campaign, so nothing else can be charged.

**These figures are not installs.** A link click means someone tapped through to junivela.app/valentine. X can't see whether they then installed the app, so only your own install totals show how the campaign did against your goal.

## Setup
- **Campaign and ad groups:** one campaign with two ad groups, each promoting one post. Both were optimised for link clicks to the landing page, since there's no pixel or app ID for X to optimise toward installs.
- **Targeting:** iPhone/iOS only and worldwide, so no budget went to people who can't install the app. I added a 13+ age floor to match X's minimum age. Your posts are already localised, so I didn't narrow by language. X's restricted-category rules don't apply to a sleep-sounds app, and both posts were approved before launch.
- **Third post not used:** I didn't promote "Why does the ceiling get all your best thoughts…" (…646911). It doesn't say the app is iPhone-only, and "a little Valentine freebie awaits" could make people expect a gift or voucher. You asked to keep that distinction clear, and the other two posts already explain it.

## Changes during the flight
- **Day 1:** the two posts cost about the same per click, so I left the even split in place.
- **Day 2:** the "send someone" post (B) was getting more clicks for the money, so I moved budget toward it. B's daily cap went from $30 to $37 and A's from $30 to $25.
- **Day 4:** B was still ahead, with about 70–84 clicks a day against about 50 for A, so I shifted again for Saturday and Sunday. B's daily cap went to $49 and A's to $19. The total of both ad groups' caps always stayed at $300.
- **Valentine's Day:** I checked mid-day and again after the flight ended. Delivery was normal and both posts stayed approved throughout.

The gift-framed post (B) brought in more clicks at a slightly lower cost per click. It would be worth checking whether your install data shows the same pattern.
