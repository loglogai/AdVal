# Bracket Bust Sweepstakes: X campaign recap

The campaign ran from Tuesday, March 30 through Monday, April 5, Central time. It finished at **$548.14**, under the $550 budget, and has now stopped serving.

| | Spend | Impressions | Link clicks | Cost per click |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Total** | **$548.14** | **472,050** | **299** | **$1.83** |
| Post …5334 ("Bracket busted by the first weekend?") | $453.59 | 343,273 | 263 | $1.72 |
| Post …4049 (prize details and eligibility rules) | $94.55 | 128,777 | 36 | $2.63 |

**Setup:** I ran one campaign with one ad group per post, shown to people 21+ in the United States. Each post was promoted as written. X optimized delivery for link clicks, since that's the closest thing it can measure to an entry without the pixel. Both posts passed X's ad review on the first try.

**Changes during the week:**
- **After 2 days (Apr 1):** The "bracket busted" post was bringing in clicks about 35% cheaper. I shifted most of the daily budget to it.
- **After 4 days (Apr 3):** The prize-details post got worse (3 clicks for $10 that day), so I paused it. All remaining budget went to the "bracket busted" post.
- **Weekend:** The "bracket busted" post did its best on Final Four weekend and championship Monday: 52, 56 and 50 clicks a day at about $1.50–1.60 each. That was cheaper than its early days.

**For matching against your entries:**
- All of these are link clicks. X can't tell how many of those people actually entered, so your entry count is the real result. Entries ÷ 299 gives your click-to-entry rate, and $548.14 ÷ entries gives your cost per entry.
- If you want, I can also pull daily figures so you can check whether entries rose on the same days as clicks.
- The cheeky, basketball-fan post clearly beat the plain prize announcement. That's worth remembering for next year. Installing X's pixel on the landing page before then would let X optimize for actual entries instead of clicks.
