## What this means, honestly
- **X cannot see your sign-ups.** There's no pixel on the site, so the 205 link clicks are the only outcome X measured. Sign-up count has to come from your own numbers — X's interface will show no conversions for this flight, and that's expected, not a failure.
- **No claim about cost per sign-up.** We can't compute it. The relevant question for you is: of the 205 clicks, how many turned into sign-ups? That conversion rate is the one number that would tell us whether the broad, cheap clicks or the keyword, intent-driven clicks were worth more.
- **The clicks were expensive for X standards** (~$2.32 each) and CTR was low. All four approved posts were text-only with a short link, and we couldn't change creative. That's the most likely driver, not the targeting.
- **Structure:** three ad groups at launch (broad ≈⅓ of daily budget, keyword, interest), all four posts promoted in each. Broad won on clicks and took a growing share; the interest test was retired after three days for underperforming; keyword intent kept a consistent budget throughout in case it converts better than its click cost suggests.
- Numbers are the final settled pull from X at campaign end; a few late-attributed clicks can nudge them slightly.

The strongest next step would be installing the X pixel before the next flight — it would let the next campaign optimise for sign-ups rather than clicks.
