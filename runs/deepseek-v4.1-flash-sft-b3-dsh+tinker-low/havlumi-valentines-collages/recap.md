**All three approved posts ran** across the broad and keyword groups (the interest group used the Valentine's post). Targeting held all flight: **iOS/iPhone only, worldwide except China**, no age or gender limits.

### What we saw
- **Broad targeting was the cheapest source of clicks** — $0.41 each — and produced 526 of the 816 link clicks on 41% of spend. It scaled notably once we raised its bid.
- **Keyword phrases had the highest CTR (0.078%) but cost ~3× more per click ($1.19).** They are the most intent-rich traffic, though we can't tell from X alone whether that intent turns into installs.
- **The interest group was the weakest** on both volume and CTR; we trimmed it mid-flight and kept it small.
- **Demand built toward the holiday.** The final day (Valentine's Day) was the strongest: 145 link clicks across the groups, after 135 on the Saturday.
- **Pacing:** we switched from AUTO to MAX bidding after day 1 and raised the broad bid as delivery plateaued. Spend finished at $518 of the $550 cap; the remaining ~$32 simply couldn't be profitably delivered at the bid levels that kept click costs sensible.

### Important caveat on "installs"
**X cannot report or optimize for installs here** — the site has no X pixel, so the 816 above are **link clicks**, not installs. A click is only a step toward the app-store handoff and completion. To know true cost per install, match your own app-reporting installs to this campaign (by date/UTM or store referrer), and treat the $0.63 average link-click cost as an upper-bound indicator only. The keyword group's higher CTR may or may not translate into a better install rate — that's the main open question to check in your own data.
